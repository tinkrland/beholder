# world

dimension: market landscape and competitors. product management function:
competitive intelligence and positioning.

## what beholder extracts

- **alternative facts**: every stated "instead of this, you could use" in
  readme, docs, comparison pages, and pasted text, with citations.
- **differentiator facts**: stated points of difference, held to evidence;
  a difference nobody demonstrated is a claim, and it is stored as one.
- **market statements**: size, category, and landscape claims from sources.
  beholder keeps the citation and the claim together; it does not verify the
  claim, and it says so.

## how beholder addresses it

- every lens carries an `alternatives_frame`: the set of things this reader
  will compare you against, whether or not you listed them. the view's
  "alternatives" section is written inside that frame, not inside yours.
- the category frame places the project in the world this reader knows; the
  same project can be "a devtool" to one lens and "a workflow replacement"
  to another, and both are honest if the facts support them.
- "what not to lead with" often lives here: the comparison that sounds good
  to you but invites a fight you lose with this reader.

## what beholder never does

- it never invents a competitor to seem informed, and never disparages one.
  competitive intelligence here means cited alternatives and framed
  differences.
- it never accepts an uncited market claim as fact. a number from a source is
  that source's number, kept in provenance, never smoothed into "the market
  is $x".
