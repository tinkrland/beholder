# what

dimension: product features and core value. product management function:
solution design and capabilities.

## what beholder extracts

- **capability facts**: what the project does, from the three extraction
  passes. the static pass reads manifests and configs; the structural pass
  reads the repo shape (routes, flows, migrations) as observed, not verified;
  the semantic pass reads readme, docs, and pasted text for stated
  capabilities.
- **mechanic facts**: how it works when a source explains the mechanism, the
  part that differentiates a design from a claim.
- **differentiator facts**: what the project says is different, held to the
  same evidence bar as everything else.
- **pricing and metric facts**: what a source states about pricing and any
  stated numbers, each with its citation.

## how beholder addresses it

- "what is different and why they care" is the center of every pitch view,
  and it ranks proof by the lens's `trusted_evidence` order.
- the overclaim finder lives closest to this dimension: a "what" that goes
  beyond the evidence is rewritten to the defensible version.
- a described feature with no wiring is stored with low confidence and
  documented-not-verified status, and it cannot anchor an export sentence
  until you confirm it.

## what beholder never does

- it never upgrades a described feature to a working feature. the static,
  structural, and semantic passes disagree honestly: the strongest claim about
  a feature is the weakest pass that mentions it, not the strongest.
- it never lets a capability sentence stand without a fact id behind it.
