# way

dimension: roadmap, horizons and scope guard. product management function:
release planning and scope management.

## what beholder extracts

- **maturity facts**: what exists now, from the repo's own shape, the
  strongest signal of where the way actually is.
- **roadmap statements**: what sources say is planned, from roadmap docs,
  changelogs, and open milestone references. stored as plans, never promoted
  to facts about the product.
- **scope statements**: what the project says it does not do, from docs and
  principles files. an explicit non-goal is a fact worth having.

## how beholder addresses it

- "what not to lead with" is the scope guard in every view: it names what
  hurts or distracts for this reader, including features too early to lead
  with and comparisons that open the wrong question.
- scoring separates today from later: a criterion the roadmap would satisfy
  but the code does not scores low, and the gap names the stage that would
  close it.
- future-facing sentences in views bind to roadmap provenance and are marked
  as plans. they never count as proof, and never anchor a claim of
  capability.

## what beholder never does

- the future is a plan, never a fact. beholder does not present roadmap
  language as current capability, and the honesty guard blocks sentences
  that blur the two.
- it never quietly drops scope discipline. a project that says it does
  everything gets that fact surfaced, not celebrated.
