# about

## why beholder exists

founders and builders spend weeks making a project and an hour describing it. descriptions get reused across audiences that judge very differently. nobody tells them what the judges will actually weight, what they will ask, or which sentence will cost them credibility.

beholder gives every builder an outside view, per audience, anchored to the audience's own criteria.

## who it is for

- hackathon builders facing a judging sheet and a sponsor track list
- early founders applying to accelerators, grants, and funds
- solo creators and developers pitching partners and customers
- anyone pitching one project to several audiences

v1 is for projects with code, full or half written: repos are the primary input, from github, gitlab, bitbucket, or gitea, and a project can span several. prose-only projects are the v2 horizon.

## principles

1. **target first.** "investors" is not a target. "yc" is. "judges" is not a target. "this hackathon's rubric" is.
2. **evidence or it does not ship.** every sentence in a pitch binds to a fact with provenance, or to the target's own criteria.
3. **gaps are the product.** fluent copy is a commodity. "you have no demo link and judges weight demo at 30 percent" is not.
4. **never invent proof.** beholder will not make up traction, metrics, or testimonials to close a gap.
5. **consistency across audiences.** one project model, many views. no pitch may contradict another.
6. **pitching, not marketing.** no campaigns, posting, scheduling, ads, seo, or brand work.
7. **private by default.** projects and applications are yours.

## what makes it hard to copy

the **lens library**: structured, sourced, dated lenses for real rubrics, programs, and partner types. a model wrapper around a rubric is easy. a trustworthy, current library of how real targets judge is not.

## what it will not do

- guarantee an outcome
- write marketing campaigns
- scrape or track individuals
- pretend a preset lens is current when it is stale
